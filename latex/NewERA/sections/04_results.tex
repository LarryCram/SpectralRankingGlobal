\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Coverage}

Table~\ref{tab:era_divisions} lists the 22 divisions with the size of each retained network and the number of rated UoEs that could be matched. At the two-digit level, 506 of the 677 rated UoEs (75\%) were matched: 253 of 282 in citation disciplines, 234 of 352 in peer review disciplines and 19 of 43 in the two mixed divisions. At the four-digit level the proportion fell to 864 of 1,923 (45\%), partly because 50 groups have no OpenAlex subfield mapped to them and partly because an institution must reach the threshold within a narrower field.

Coverage was lost mainly in the humanities, law and the creative arts. In Law and Legal Studies (18) only 24 sources and 52 institutions worldwide met the thresholds, the spectral gap was 0.05, and 5 of 33 rated UoEs could be matched. History and Archaeology (21) and Philosophy and Religious Studies (22) had spectral gaps of 0.07 and 0.03, and Studies in Creative Arts and Writing (19) matched 8 of 35 UoEs. In these divisions the reference network among works indexed by OpenAlex is too sparse, or too weakly connected, to support a ranking, consistent with the known shortfall of citation indexes in these fields \citep{mongeonJournalCoverageWeb2016}. Technology (10) is a different case: its subfields are distributed across other divisions by the crosswalk, and only one Australian institution reached the threshold. Elsewhere, all divisions retained more than 200 sources and institutions, with spectral gaps between 0.13 and 0.77.

Unmatched UoEs were not a random subset. Their mean ERA rating was 3.18, against 3.79 for matched UoEs, and 33\% of them were rated 1 or 2, against 10\% of matched UoEs. Smaller units, which are more likely to fall below the threshold in OpenAlex, were also more likely to be rated below world standard. The agreement statistics below therefore describe the better-rated part of the exercise.

\subsection{Agreement with ERA ratings}

Within divisions, the Spearman correlation between $v$ and the ERA rating was positive in 20 of the 21 divisions where it could be computed (Table~\ref{tab:era_divisions}). The exception, Law, rests on five UoEs. Correlations were highest in Biological Sciences ($r_s = 0.79$), Commerce, Management, Tourism and Services (0.79), Mathematical Sciences (0.79) and Economics (0.72), Among divisions with more than ten matched UoEs, they were lowest in Built Environment and Design (0.08), Agricultural and Veterinary Sciences (0.30), Language, Communication and Culture (0.31) and Environmental Sciences (0.32). With at most 39 UoEs per division the bootstrap intervals are wide, and in eight divisions they include zero.

Pooling percentile ranks within divisions gives $r_s = 0.53$ over 253 UoEs in citation disciplines and $r_s = 0.51$ over 234 UoEs in peer review disciplines. The spectral ranking therefore agreed about as well with ratings that ERA committees based on peer review as with ratings based on citation indicators. Two of the closest agreements, Economics and Information and Computing Sciences ($r_s = 0.68$), were in disciplines assessed by peer review. At the four-digit level, agreement was lower in citation disciplines ($r_s = 0.26$, 592 UoEs in 60 groups) than in peer review disciplines ($r_s = 0.50$, 272 UoEs in 28 groups).

Figure~\ref{fig:v_by_rating} shows how the ratings were distributed against $v$. In citation disciplines the median $\log_{10} v$ rises monotonically with the rating, from $-0.45$ at rating~1 to $0.08$ at rating~5. In peer review disciplines the medians also rise with the rating, from $-0.46$ to $0.00$, but the spread within each rating is wider. Five of the eight UoEs with $v$ below a third of the world mean that were nevertheless rated 4 or 5 were in Law, where the network is too sparse to rank.

\subsection{Calibration against world standard}

ERA ratings are framed relative to world standard, and $v = 1$ is the output-weighted world mean of a field, so the two scales can be compared directly, although they are not the same quantity. In citation disciplines, 49\% of matched UoEs had $v \ge 1$, and 93\% of these were rated above world standard (4 or 5). Of UoEs with $v < 1$, 70\% were also rated above world standard. In peer review disciplines, 32\% of matched UoEs had $v \ge 1$; 59\% of these and 33\% of those with $v < 1$ were rated above world standard. Relative to the spectral ranking, ERA ratings were higher overall, and the difference was largest in citation disciplines. The proportion of Australian UoEs at or above the world mean varied widely between divisions, from none in Economics and 9\% in Psychology and Cognitive Sciences to 82\% in Engineering (Table~\ref{tab:era_divisions}).

\subsection{Institutional characteristics at equal influence}

Table~\ref{tab:era_premium} reports linear models of the ERA rating of two-digit UoEs, with division fixed effects and standard errors clustered by institution. In citation disciplines, a doubling of $v$ was associated with a rating higher by 0.91 points ($3.01 \times \log_{10} 2$) when $v$ was the only regressor. In peer review disciplines the corresponding difference was 0.50 points.

Adding membership of the Group of Eight (Go8), the association of eight research-intensive universities, changes the picture differently in the two kinds of discipline. In citation disciplines, Go8 units were rated 0.35 points (s.e. 0.11) higher than other units with the same $v$, and the coefficient on $\log_{10} v$ fell modestly, from 3.01 to 2.50. In peer review disciplines, Go8 units were rated 0.97 points (s.e. 0.20) higher at the same $v$, and the coefficient on $\log_{10} v$ fell by about half, from 1.67 to 0.89. In peer review disciplines, therefore, the difference in ratings between Go8 and other institutions was about as large as the difference associated with a tenfold difference in $v$. Excluding the five sparse divisions (10, 18, 19, 21 and 22) leaves the result substantially unchanged: the Go8 coefficient in peer review disciplines, in the model that also includes committee representation, is 0.89 (s.e. 0.20) over 202 UoEs, against 0.95 in the full sample, and the pooled within-division correlation is $r_s = 0.54$.
