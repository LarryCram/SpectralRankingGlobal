\section{Results}

\subsection{Coverage}

Table~\ref{tab:era_divisions} lists the 22 divisions with the size of each retained network and the number of rated UoEs that could be matched. At the two-digit level, 513 of the 677 rated UoEs (76\%) were matched: 256 of 282 in citation disciplines, 237 of 352 in peer review disciplines and 20 of 43 in the two mixed divisions. At the four-digit level the proportion fell to 898 of 1,923 (47\%), partly because 50 groups have no OpenAlex subfield mapped to them and partly because an institution must reach the threshold within a narrower field.

Coverage was lost mainly in the humanities, law and the creative arts. In Law and Legal Studies (18) only 24 sources and 52 institutions worldwide met the thresholds, the spectral gap was 0.05, and 5 of 33 rated UoEs could be matched. History and Archaeology (21) and Philosophy and Religious Studies (22) had spectral gaps of 0.08 and 0.03, and Studies in Creative Arts and Writing (19) matched 8 of 35 UoEs. In these divisions the reference network among works indexed by OpenAlex is too sparse, or too weakly connected, to support a ranking, consistent with the known shortfall of citation indexes in these fields \citep{mongeonJournalCoverageWeb2016}. Technology (10) is a different case: its subfields are distributed across other divisions by the crosswalk, and only two Australian institutions reached the threshold. Elsewhere, all divisions retained more than 240 sources and 440 institutions, with spectral gaps between 0.22 and 0.77.

Unmatched UoEs were not a random subset. Their mean ERA rating was 3.17, against 3.79 for matched UoEs, and 32\% of them were rated 1 or 2, against 10\% of matched UoEs. Smaller units, which are more likely to fall below the threshold in OpenAlex, were also more likely to be rated below world standard. The agreement statistics below therefore describe the better-rated part of the exercise.

\subsection{Influence against rank within an emulated unit}

Figure~\ref{fig:facet08} shows the form in which the emulation presents its results, for Information and Computing Sciences (08), a division that ERA assessed by peer review. The top panel plots $\log_{10} v$ of each of the 2,675 institutions retained in the division against its global rank by $v$. The curve falls steeply over the first few dozen institutions, flattens through a long middle in which most institutions lie within a factor of two of the world mean, and steepens again in the tail. The 34 Australian HEPs retained in the division are marked by their ERA 2018 rating. They occupy ranks between about 130 and 1,600, and their order along the curve broadly follows their ratings: the four units rated~5 lie among the first 430 institutions, all ten units rated~4 lie above rank 920, the four units rated~2 lie below rank 1,000, and the units rated~3 are spread from rank 440 to the tail. The Spearman correlation over the 33 rated UoEs is 0.75.

The lower panels show the four-digit groups to which OpenAlex subfields map, with each institution placed at its rank in the division, so that a vertical line follows one institution down the figure. In the three largest groups, the group $v$ of an Australian unit differs from its division $v$ by a median factor of 1.1 to 1.2 and at most a factor of three, and in the smaller groups the world-wide scatter about the division curve is wider. Computer Software (0803) retained 101 institutions worldwide and one in Australia, so a four-digit UoE that ERA rated may have no counterpart in the emulation even when the division is well covered. The stars, which show $v$ computed with references to and from works outside the field, are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:res_sentinel}. Equivalent figures for all 22 divisions are given in the Supplementary Material.

\subsection{Agreement with ERA ratings}

Within divisions, the Spearman correlation between $v$ and the ERA rating was positive in 20 of the 21 divisions where it could be computed (Table~\ref{tab:era_divisions}). The exception, Law, rests on five UoEs. Correlations were highest in Biological Sciences ($r_s = 0.80$), Commerce, Management, Tourism and Services (0.80), Mathematical Sciences (0.77), Information and Computing Sciences (0.75) and Economics (0.70). Among divisions with more than ten matched UoEs, they were lowest in Built Environment and Design (0.11), Agricultural and Veterinary Sciences (0.30), Environmental Sciences (0.33) and Language, Communication and Culture (0.33). With at most 39 UoEs per division the bootstrap intervals are wide, and in eight divisions they include zero.

Pooling percentile ranks within divisions gives $r_s = 0.53$ over 256 UoEs in citation disciplines and $r_s = 0.54$ over 237 UoEs in peer review disciplines. The spectral ranking therefore agreed as well with ratings that ERA committees based on peer review as with ratings based on citation indicators. Three of the five closest agreements, Commerce, Information and Computing Sciences and Economics, were in disciplines assessed by peer review. At the four-digit level, agreement was lower in citation disciplines ($r_s = 0.27$, 604 UoEs in 60 groups) than in peer review disciplines ($r_s = 0.53$, 294 UoEs in 31 groups).

Figure~\ref{fig:divisions} sets six divisions side by side on a common scale: the two citation disciplines with the highest and lowest agreement among the larger divisions (Biological Sciences, 0.80; Environmental Sciences, 0.33), the largest division (Medical and Health Sciences, 0.53), two peer review disciplines that differ in the same way (Economics, 0.70; Language, Communication and Culture, 0.33), and Law, where the network is too sparse to rank. The panels differ as much in where the Australian units sit on the curve as in the order of ratings along it. In Biological Sciences the Australian units occupy the upper and middle parts of a curve of about 3,000 institutions, and most units rated~5 rank above those rated~4. In Medical and Health Sciences the 39 units are spread from rank 470 to rank 5,000 of 7,400, and units rated 4 and 5 are interleaved through the middle of the curve. In Environmental Sciences 70\% of the Australian units lie above the world mean, but units rated 3, 4 and 5 are interleaved along the whole range. In Economics all but one of the Australian units lie below the world mean, yet the ratings are well ordered: the eight highest-ranked units were rated 4 or 5, and of the 15 below them only one was rated~5 and none~4. In Law the five matched units lie in the steep tail of a curve of 52 institutions.

\subsection{Calibration against world standard}

ERA ratings are framed relative to world standard, and $v = 1$ is the output-weighted world mean of a field, so the two scales can be compared directly, although they are not the same quantity. In citation disciplines, 48\% of matched UoEs had $v \ge 1$, and 94\% of these were rated above world standard (4 or 5). Of UoEs with $v < 1$, 69\% were also rated above world standard. In peer review disciplines, 32\% of matched UoEs had $v \ge 1$; 59\% of these and 32\% of those with $v < 1$ were rated above world standard. Relative to the spectral ranking, ERA ratings were higher overall, and the difference was largest in citation disciplines. The proportion of Australian UoEs at or above the world mean varied widely between divisions, from 4\% in Economics and 8\% in Studies in Human Society to 77\% in Earth Sciences (Table~\ref{tab:era_divisions}).

\subsection{Institutional characteristics at equal influence}

Table~\ref{tab:era_premium} reports linear models of the ERA rating of two-digit UoEs, with division fixed effects and standard errors clustered by institution. In citation disciplines, a doubling of $v$ was associated with a rating higher by 0.89 points ($2.96 \times \log_{10} 2$) when $v$ was the only regressor. In peer review disciplines the corresponding difference was 0.55 points.

Adding membership of the Group of Eight (Go8), the association of eight research-intensive universities, changes the picture differently in the two kinds of discipline. In citation disciplines, Go8 units were rated 0.35 points (s.e. 0.11) higher than other units with the same $v$, and the coefficient on $\log_{10} v$ fell modestly, from 2.96 to 2.45. In peer review disciplines, Go8 units were rated 0.96 points (s.e. 0.20) higher at the same $v$, and the coefficient on $\log_{10} v$ fell by almost half, from 1.84 to 1.04. In peer review disciplines, therefore, the difference in ratings between Go8 and other institutions was about as large as the difference associated with an eightfold difference in $v$. Excluding the five sparse divisions (10, 18, 19, 21 and 22) leaves the result substantially unchanged: the Go8 coefficient in peer review disciplines, in the model that also includes committee representation, is 0.88 (s.e. 0.20) over 205 UoEs, against 0.94 in the full sample, and the pooled within-division correlation is $r_s = 0.57$.

\subsection{References from outside the field}
\label{sec:res_sentinel}

The stars in Figures~\ref{fig:facet08} and~\ref{fig:divisions} show each Australian unit's $v$ when the ranking also counts references that cross the boundary of the emulated field ($\varepsilon = 1$). The sentinel changes the ranking of institutions worldwide only moderately: the Spearman correlation between $v$ with and without it has a median of 0.83 over the 22 divisions, and falls to 0.42 only in Law, where 19 institutions join the network through the sentinel. It moves Australian units much more systematically. Over the 531 Australian two-digit units retained in both rankings, $v$ rose in 73\%, with a median rise of 0.08 in $\log_{10} v$ (interquartile range $-0.01$ to 0.21), and the share at or above the world mean rose from 40\% to 60\%. Among matched UoEs it rose from 48\% to 71\% in citation disciplines and from 32\% to 46\% in peer review disciplines, so that with the sentinel the spectral ranking comes closer to ERA's view of the level of Australian research. The rise was largest in Law (median 0.79), Studies in Creative Arts and Writing (0.28) and Economics (0.23), and in Figure~\ref{fig:divisions} it is visible in Economics as a column of stars above the lower-ranked units.

The rise was not shared evenly. Its median was 0.35 for UoEs rated~1, 0.23 for UoEs rated~2 and 0.01 for UoEs rated~5, and 0.12 for units outside the Go8 against 0.02 for Go8 units. References from outside the field therefore compress the range of $v$ among Australian units, and they compress it towards the units that ERA rated lower. Agreement with ERA ratings falls accordingly: the pooled within-division correlation drops from 0.53 to 0.20 in citation disciplines and from 0.54 to 0.39 in peer review disciplines, and at equal $v$ the Go8 coefficient rises from 0.35 to 0.67 (s.e. 0.10) in citation disciplines and from 0.96 to 1.11 (s.e. 0.20) in peer review disciplines. ERA ratings track the reception of work within its field more closely than its reception from outside.

\subsection{Assignment of works to Fields of Research}
\label{sec:res_journal}

The emulation assigns works to FoR codes through a crosswalk from OpenAlex subfields, whereas ERA 2018 assigned journal articles through the codes of the journal in which they appeared. When the emulation is repeated with journal-list assignment (Section~\ref{sec:data}), 136 rather than 107 four-digit groups can be emulated, and the number of matched UoEs rises from 513 to 555 at the two-digit level and from 898 to 1,061 at the four-digit level. The gains are concentrated where the crosswalk had little to work with: matched UoEs rose from 2 to 12 in Technology, from 5 to 13 in Law and from 12 to 20 in Agricultural and Veterinary Sciences. Among the four-digit medical groups, Human Movement and Sports Science (1106) went from no matched UoEs to 17, and Nursing (1110) from 1 to 24.

Agreement and the institutional association change little. The pooled within-division correlation is 0.55 in citation disciplines and 0.52 in peer review disciplines (crosswalk: 0.53 and 0.54), and at the four-digit level 0.30 and 0.47 (0.27 and 0.53). Agreement improved in Physical Sciences (0.51 to 0.65), Chemical Sciences (0.41 to 0.56) and Environmental Sciences (0.33 to 0.44), and fell in Mathematical Sciences (0.77 to 0.64), Commerce (0.80 to 0.68) and Language, Communication and Culture (0.33 to 0.09). The pooled correlation in the two mixed divisions fell from 0.65 to 0.26, because the ten Technology units that journal assignment adds disagree with their ratings ($r_s = -0.31$ over 12 units). At equal $v$ the Go8 coefficient is 0.30 (s.e. 0.10) in citation disciplines and 0.95 (s.e. 0.18) in peer review disciplines, against 0.35 and 0.96 under the crosswalk. The main results do not depend on the method of assignment.
