\section{ERA 2018 and Its Emulation}
\label{sec:data}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{ERA 2018}

ERA 2018 evaluated research outputs published by Australian higher education providers in 2011--2016 (journal articles; for some disciplines also books, chapters, conference papers and non-traditional outputs). Outputs were assigned by the institutions to codes of the 2008 Australian and New Zealand Standard Research Classification Fields of Research (FoR 2008), which has 22 two-digit divisions and 157 four-digit groups. A UoE is an institution's output in one two-digit or four-digit code. A UoE was evaluated only if it exceeded a low-volume threshold of 50 outputs, counted either as indexed journal articles (for disciplines assessed by citation analysis) or as weighted outputs, with books weighted five (for disciplines assessed by peer review). Eight Research Evaluation Committees (RECs) rated each UoE on a five-point scale, from 1 (well below world standard) to 5 (well above world standard), with 3 denoting world standard.

The ERA 2018 Discipline Matrix fixed, for each FoR code, whether the committee drew principally on citation indicators supplied by the Australian Research Council or on the reports of peer reviewers who read a sample of nominated outputs. Of the 22 divisions, nine were assessed by citation analysis, eleven by peer review, and two (01 Mathematical Sciences and 10 Technology) were mixed, with different methods for different four-digit groups (Table~\ref{tab:era_divisions}). Information and Computing Sciences (08) and Economics (14) were assessed by peer review. The published outcomes contain 2,600 rated UoEs from 40 institutions: 677 at the two-digit level and 1,923 at the four-digit level.

\subsection{Corpus}

The corpus is the October 2026 snapshot of OpenAlex \citep{priemOpenAlexFullyopenIndex2022}. We retain works of nine types (article, review, book, book chapter, conference paper, dissertation, letter, preprint and report) that are not retracted, are not paratext, have at least one reference, and have no more than 29 authors; the author cap excludes consortium papers whose credit cannot reasonably be apportioned among institutions. Works with the same normalised title are counted once, so that a preprint and its published version do not both enter the network. Institutions are restricted to the OpenAlex types education, nonprofit, government, healthcare and other. For the emulation, the target and census windows coincide with the ERA 2018 reference period, $t_t = t_c = [2011, 2016]$: references are taken from works published in 2011--2016 to works published in 2011--2016.

\subsection{Emulating units of evaluation}

OpenAlex classifies each work into topics, and each topic belongs to one of 252 subfields. We map each OpenAlex subfield to a single FoR 2008 group using a published crosswalk (\texttt{ResearchClassification}; % TODO(authors): citation / URL for the crosswalk
), and a division is the union of its groups. Because the mapping is many-to-one, a four-digit group that no subfield maps to cannot be emulated; 107 of the 157 groups are reachable. For each division and reachable group we compute a separate spectral ranking of all sources and institutions worldwide, using the field weights $f_{wF}$ of Section~\ref{sec:spectral_ranking}.

The thresholds follow ERA. An institution is retained in a unit if its field-weighted, author-fractional output over the six years is at least 50, so that $\tau_I = 50/6$ works per year matches the low-volume threshold. A source is retained if it published at least 60 field-weighted works ($\tau_S = 10$ per year). ERA assigned multidisciplinary journals such as \textit{Nature}, \textit{Science} and \textit{PLOS ONE} to a separate category (MD) of its journal list; the 708 OpenAlex sources matched to that category are retained in any unit in which they published at least one field-weighted work, so that their articles are not lost when no single field reaches $\tau_S$. All rankings use the baseline parameters of Section~\ref{sec:spectral_ranking}: bipartite mask, fractional reference counting ($\rho = 0$), author-fractional institutional credit and no sentinel ($\varepsilon = 0$).

Each Australian institution is identified with its OpenAlex institution record through a concordance maintained for this study, and a rated UoE is matched when its institution is retained in the corresponding emulated unit. The comparison statistic is the Spearman correlation $r_s$ between $v$ and the ERA rating across the matched UoEs of a code. Its 95\% interval is obtained from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of UoEs. To pool codes, we convert $v$ and the rating to percentile ranks within each code before correlating, which removes differences between disciplines in the level of ratings.
