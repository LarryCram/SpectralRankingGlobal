\section{Mathematical Framework: Joint Spectral Ranking}
\label{sec:spectral_ranking}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

We apply the recirculating spectral ranking model of \citet{docampodomingoRecirculatingCardinalSpectral2026} to selected works published by a set of countries $C \in \mathfrak{C}$, classified by research field $F \in \mathfrak{F}$. We distinguish two time intervals: the target window $t_t = [t_0^t, t_1^t]$, which specifies the publication years of the selected works $\mathfrak{W}^t$, and the census window $t_c = [t_0^c, t_1^c]$, which specifies the publication years of the referencing works $\mathfrak{W}^c$ whose reference lists are examined. 

Unlike \citet{docampodomingoRecirculatingCardinalSpectral2026}, sources are not pre-selected from field-specific journal registries; instead, both sources and institutions enter field $F$ solely by publishing a minimum volume of field-weighted works within the window.  The works $w \in \mathfrak{W}^t$ with weight $f_{wF}$ in field $F$ select candidate sources $\mathfrak{S}'$ and, through author affiliations, candidate institutions $\mathfrak{I}'$. Then sources and institutions whose accumulated field-weighted works meet annual thresholds $\tau_S$ and $\tau_I$ are retained in the surviving sets $\mathfrak{S} \subseteq \mathfrak{S}'$ ($|\mathfrak{S}| = N_S$) and $\mathfrak{I} \subseteq \mathfrak{I}'$ ($|\mathfrak{I}| = N_I$). This work-level classification accommodates multidisciplinary journals directly, allowing any journal to participate in each field where its field-weighted output reaches $\tau_S$ without requiring journal-level disciplinary assignment. Works with source works-count below $\tau_S$, or institutional affiliation-count under $\tau_I$, are removed from the corpus, leaving $N_w$ retained works $\mathfrak{W}$. From the reference lists of census works $\mathfrak{W}^c$ to the retained target works $\mathfrak{W}^t$, we form the filtered reference set $\mathfrak{R} = \{r(i, j) : w_i \in \mathfrak{W}^c,\, w_j \in \mathfrak{W}^t\}$, where work $w_i$ references work $w_j$ and $w_j$ is cited by $w_i$ \citep{woutersCitationCulture1999}. Some referencing works lie outside $F$, or give references to works outside $F$. The referencing practices of exogenous works can be ignored ($\varepsilon = 0$) or modelled by a single fictitious sentinel work for field $F$ that gives and received references from the exogenous works  ($\varepsilon = 1$).   

Each reference $r(i, j) \in \mathfrak{R}$ is weighted by field relevance, reference-list length, and institutional authorship share. First, the reference carries the mean field weight of the referencing and referenced works, $f_{ij,F} = \frac{1}{2}(f_{iF} + f_{jF})$. Second, let $R_i = \sum_{j : (i,j) \in \mathfrak{R}} f_{ij,F}$ be the field-weighted within-corpus reference count of work $w_i$, and $\bar{R} = \sum_{i} R_i / N_w$ be its mean over $\mathfrak{W}$. Under fractional reference counting ($\rho = 0$), each reference from $w_i$ is weighted by $\rho_i = \bar{R} / R_i$, ensuring that every work's filtered reference list counts equally \citep{zittModifyingJournalImpact2008}. Third, while each work links to a unique source in $\mathfrak{S}$, its credit across institutions in $\mathfrak{I}$ is fractionated by author-fractional counting \citep{waltmanFieldnormalizedCitationImpact2015}:
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{ix} = \sum_{\ell :\, x \in \mathcal{I}_{i\ell}} \frac{1}{a_i\, x_{i\ell}},
    \label{eq:inst_weight}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}_{i\ell}$ is the set of institutions of author $\ell$ of work $w_i$, $x_{i\ell} = |\mathcal{I}_{i\ell}|$ is its cardinality, and $a_i$ is the author count of $w_i$. Authorships without an institution are excluded, and institution pruning at $\tau_I$ precedes fractional counting.

With works and references defined in this way, we construct the indexed set of units $\mathfrak{P} = \mathfrak{S} \cup \mathfrak{I}$ with $N = N_S + N_I$ elements \citep{pinskiCitationInfluenceJournal1976}, ordered so that sources occupy indices $1, \dots, N_S$ and institutions occupy indices $N_S + 1, \dots, N$. For each work $w_i$, we define the source membership one-hot vector $\mathbf{b}_S(i) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_S}$ with $[\mathbf{b}_S(i)]_x = \mathbf{1}[w_i \in x]$ and the institution weight vector $\mathbf{b}_I(i) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_I}$ with $[\mathbf{b}_I(i)]_y = \omega_{iy}$ from (\ref{eq:inst_weight}). Adopting the bipartite block mask $\mathbf{m} = (m_{SS}, m_{SI}, m_{IS}, m_{II}) = (0110)$ with unit balance $\chi = 0.5$ \citep{docampodomingoRecirculatingCardinalSpectral2026}, references over $\mathfrak{R}$ link sources and institutions across two block matrices accumulated via rank-1 outer products:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{C}_{SI} = \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathfrak{R}} f_{ij,F}\, \rho_i\, \mathbf{b}_S(i)\, \mathbf{b}_I(j)^\top, \qquad
    \mathbf{C}_{IS} = \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathfrak{R}} f_{ij,F}\, \rho_i\, \mathbf{b}_I(i)\, \mathbf{b}_S(j)^\top.
    \label{eq:c_blocks}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf{b}_X(i)$ projects the referencing side over units of type $X$ and $\mathbf{b}_Y(j)$ projects the referenced side over units of type $Y$, so rows index references given and columns index citations received. Let $r_p = \sum_q C_{pq}$ be the row sum of fractionated references given by unit $p$ and $\mathbf{D}_r = \mathrm{diag}(r_p)$ the corresponding diagonal matrix. Row-normalising the partitioned reference matrix $\mathbf{C}$ gives the transition matrix
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{D}_r^{-1} \mathbf{C} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{H}_{SI} \\ \mathbf{H}_{IS} & \mathbf{0} \end{pmatrix},
    \label{eq:h_bipartite}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{H}_{SI}$ connects sources to institutions and $\mathbf{H}_{IS}$ connects institutions to sources, removing differences in reference intensity across units when $\rho = 0$.

Because every path in $\mathbf{H}$ alternates $S \to I \to S \to \cdots$, $\mathbf{H}$ is periodic with period 2 and thus imprimitive. Squaring $\mathbf{H}$ collapses two-step paths onto separate unit types \citep{breigerDualityPersonsGroups1974}, yielding the one-mode operators
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H}^2 = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{M}_S & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{M}_I \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
    \mathbf{M}_S = \mathbf{H}_{SI}\mathbf{H}_{IS}, \qquad
    \mathbf{M}_I = \mathbf{H}_{IS}\mathbf{H}_{SI}.
    \label{eq:breiger}
\end{equation}
In the baseline unattenuated regime ($\alpha = 1$, $\boldsymbol{\mu} = \mathbf{0}$, corresponding to the $\alpha \to 1$ limit of the Katz--Hubbell resolvent; \citealp{katzNewStatusIndex1953, hubbellInputoutputApproachClique1965, vignaSpectralRanking2016}), the source ranking vector $\boldsymbol{\pi}_S$ satisfies the Perron--Frobenius equation
\begin{equation}
    (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}_S^\top)\boldsymbol{\pi}_S = \mathbf{0}, \qquad \mathbf{1}^\top\boldsymbol{\pi}_S = 1, \qquad \boldsymbol{\pi}_S \ge \mathbf{0},
    \label{eq:pf_sources}
\end{equation}
and the institution ranking vector follows directly as the matrix--vector product
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\pi}_I = \mathbf{H}_{SI}^\top \boldsymbol{\pi}_S, \qquad \mathbf{1}^\top\boldsymbol{\pi}_I = 1.
    \label{eq:pf_insts}
\end{equation}
When $\mathbf{M}_S$ is primitive, $\boldsymbol{\pi}_S$ and $\boldsymbol{\pi}_I$ are unique by the Perron--Frobenius theorem. As \citet{gellerCitationInfluenceMethodology1978} showed, $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ is the stationary distribution of the Markov chain and coincides with the influence weight of \citet{pinskiCitationInfluenceJournal1976} scaled by the reference-count proportion $r_p / \sum_q r_q$.

From the ranking probability $\pi_p$ of unit $p$, the size-independent influence $v_p$ is computed as \citep{palacios-huertaMeasurementIntellectualInfluence2004}
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v} = A\, \mathbf{A}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\pi}, \qquad \mathbf{A} = \mathrm{diag}(a_p), \qquad A = \mathbf{a}^\top \mathbf{1} = \sum_p a_p,
    \label{eq:influence_v}
\end{equation}
that is, $v_p = (A / a_p) \pi_p$, where $a_p = \sum_{w_i \in p} f_{iF}\, \omega_{ip}$ (with $\omega_{ip} = 1$ for $p \in \mathfrak{S}$) is the field-weighted work count of unit $p$ and $A$ is the total weighted work count of the unit set. The scalar $A$ normalises $\mathbf{v}$ so that its $a_p$-weighted mean equals one: $\mathbf{a}^\top \mathbf{v} / A = \mathbf{1}^\top \boldsymbol{\pi} = 1$. This normalisation provides interpretable cardinal values \citep{stevensTheoryScalesMeasurement1946}: a unit with $v_p = 1$ has the corpus-average influence, and $v_p = 2$ indicates twice the corpus-average influence, corresponding to influence per publication \citep{pinskiCitationInfluenceJournal1976} and the article influence score \citep{bergstromEigenfactorMetrics2008}. \citet{palacios-huertaMeasurementIntellectualInfluence2004} show that $\mathbf{v}$ is uniquely characterised by invariance axioms, although \citet{serranoMeasurementIntellectualInfluence2004} provides an impossibility result via an additional axiom that breaks uniqueness.

Each field realisation produces a unit--unit reference network with one giant strongly connected component (SCC) and occasionally small disconnected components. We retain the giant SCC, drop disconnected components, and iteratively remove any units whose row sum (dangling nodes) or column sum (unreferenced nodes) on the SCC is zero until $\mathbf{H}$ is strictly row-stochastic. In our implementation, $\mathbf{H}_{SI}$ and $\mathbf{H}_{IS}$ are sparse matrices constructed from the filtered and weighted edge list $\mathcal{E}$ accumulated over $\mathfrak{R}$ in compressed sparse row (CSR) format. The dominant eigenpair of $\mathbf{M}_S^\top$ is computed with \texttt{scipy.sparse.linalg.eigs}, which also returns the next eigenvalues to verify primitivity (a unique eigenvalue of modulus $\lambda_1 = 1$ and spectral gap $1 - |\lambda_2| > 0$), after which the influence scaling (\ref{eq:influence_v}) is applied to $\boldsymbol{\pi}_S$ and $\boldsymbol{\pi}_I$.
