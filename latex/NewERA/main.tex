\begin{abstract}
\section{}
National research assessment exercises rely on expert panels whose judgements are costly to obtain and difficult to audit, while the bibliometric indicators that might support them are criticised for size bias, field dependence and dependence on journal-level proxies. We test whether a size-independent spectral ranking of institutions, computed from an open citation index, reproduces the outcomes of a completed national exercise. The ranking treats publication sources and institutions as two kinds of node in a single reference network, so that the influence of each is determined by the influence of the other, and expresses each institution's influence per unit of output relative to the world mean of its field. Using OpenAlex works published in 2011--2016, we emulate the units of evaluation of Excellence in Research for Australia (ERA) 2018 at the two-digit and four-digit levels of the 2008 Fields of Research classification, with volume thresholds matching the ERA low-volume threshold. Of 677 rated two-digit units, 513 could be emulated. Within divisions, the rank correlation between spectral influence and the ERA rating was positive in 20 of 21 divisions and of similar magnitude in disciplines that ERA assessed by citation analysis and by peer review. Coverage, not agreement, limited the method: in law, creative arts and history the network was too sparse to support a ranking. Assigning works to fields through the ERA journal list rather than a subject crosswalk raised coverage but left agreement unchanged, while counting references from outside each field raised the influence of lower-rated units most and weakened agreement. At equal spectral influence, units in Group of Eight universities received higher ratings, by about one third of a rating point in citation disciplines and one point in peer review disciplines. Representation on the rating committee added little once Group of Eight membership was taken into account. We discuss what these results imply for the bibliometric component of the assessment that is to replace ERA.
